# Product design methods

Use the current task, real artifacts and permitted evidence. Do not copy an external design skill wholesale or turn current colors, components, models or dimensions into principles. Find mutable paths and integration state through [navigation](../../../PROJECT_NAVIGATION.md). Find stable experience agreements and evidence distinctions in the [product contract](../../../docs/PRODUCT_CONTRACT.md). A method description is not evidence of implementation.

## Choose the depth

Identify the person's situation, desired outcome and whether the task is exploration, experience design, authorized implementation or a focused review. A clear small repair requires only relevant methods. Consult [project foundations](../project-foundations/SKILL.md) for major tradeoffs. Natural language remains the main entry under current strategy; replacing an implementation does not quietly abandon that strategy.

## Select methods by the problem

| Method | When and what to change | Observation and alternative |
| --- | --- | --- |
| M1 · Obtain useful help first | When registration, connection or personalization blocks starting, reduce required first-turn input to what changes the immediate result. | Walk through a task without a profile and with a partly declined permission. If authentication or a key fact is essential, explain and obtain it; do not substitute a fake result. |
| M2 · Reveal information for the task | When connection, settings, sources or internal steps compete for attention, establish the main content and next action, then group and disclose. | Keep state, sources and exits discoverable. Frequent comparisons or substantial structured editing may need lists or direct manipulation rather than every result inside a bubble. |
| M3 · Separate suggestion, acceptance and execution | When recommendation, saving, local completion and external booking can be confused, align wording, object and actual receipt. | Inspect unconnected, unauthorized, prepared, accepted, running, unknown and completed states. Describe an unimplemented state as a contract rather than displaying invented success. |
| M4 · Preserve the work in hand | When navigation, retry, cancellation, failure or text composition might lose work, assign drafts and attachments to the correct conversation and keep feedback beside the trigger. | Use synthetic material to inspect retry attachment identity, return focus, cancellation and timeout. Choose an equally recoverable platform mechanism rather than copying a particular component. |
| M5 · Separate writing space from reading comfort | When a larger window leaves content tiny or a long reply becomes too wide, inspect composer space, reading measure, type size and rhythm independently. | View the normal window, full screen and real long content. Current dimensions do not transfer blindly to a phone, another display or a denser task. |
| M6 · Motion explains change | Use brief interruptible feedback when expansion, collapse, waiting or a transition otherwise loses the person. | Operate the sequence, including reduced motion, keyboard, stop and focus. A snapshot proves a static frame only. Omit motion with no clear benefit; one easing curve is not a platform principle. |
| M7 · Personalization remains changeable and proportionate | When preferences, present emotions, official records and persona tendencies are confused, assign the correct owner before choosing display and update behavior. | Compare “not today,” a lasting dislike, “stop reminding me,” and an official recorded completion. A skill does not make an unimplemented update chain real. |
| M8 · Evidence determines refinement and stopping | When a plausible first version lacks runtime observation, comparison or feedback, separate observation, interpretation and hypotheses. | Within permission and tools: run, capture, actually inspect, use the important task, repair and recheck. For document-only work read existing evidence. Keep unobserved states explicit; subjective low-impact preferences do not require endless redesign. |

## Evidence and revision

Retain just enough traceability for an important recommendation: source requirement, problem, tradeoff, artifact, protected principle, evidence level and conditions. Distinguish traceable but unadopted guidance, confirmed adoption, observed local effect, actual feedback/comparison and a new unverified proposal. Code adoption does not demonstrate improvement; a genuine problem report does not demonstrate satisfaction after the fix.

Past redesign rounds, platform inspirations, window controls and dimensions belong to their tasks and implementations. To revise a method, state the new situation, counterexample and evidence, then update the contract and relevant observation. A method revision must not quietly change the mission.

## Completion

State the relevant methods used, the person's situation that changed, what was actually viewed, operated or run, and missing evidence. Distinguish design requirement, current implementation and current observation. Check known navigation gaps. Interface, skill and system-prompt text alone do not implement minimization, forgetting or authorization.

## Front-of-house charter

These rules encode product experience; they grant no runtime permissions and do not override P1–P5.

### Begin with subtraction

People come to advance an ordinary thought or part of their day, not to read the implementation. For each screen and state, decide what the person needs to know now. Give the main object a visual focus, make its primary action clear, and keep an exit or recovery path. Several equally weighted labels, statuses, buttons and explanations in one row are a reason to examine the structure, not a universal numerical prohibition. Consolidate secondary material into a useful details entry rather than shrinking type, adding colors or creating more cards.

### Speak in human terms

Internal lifecycle labels such as scope, epoch, receipt, candidate, provider, coverage and source ID stay out of the default main content. When someone examines a source, corrects understanding, investigates an unknown result or changes data, explain the relevant facts naturally. For example: “What helps with your answer now,” “One more thing to decide,” “Saved on this device; you can undo it,” or “The result is still uncertain; check the original service.” These are examples of truthful meanings, not compulsory scripts.

Keep necessary state, sources, errors, permissions and undo available. Simplicity comes from useful disclosure, not concealing facts.

### Memory's first question

The first screen answers **What will help me now?** Show currently useful understanding in relation to present life. Put items awaiting confirmation, disabled items and material awaiting organization behind one secondary entry, with grouping there. Start each item with its meaning; disclose conditions, time, source and correction/deletion controls on demand. Do not make a passing mood, single exception or third person's information a prominent permanent label. Use a plain invitation such as “Tell Anima something,” without requiring knowledge of assertions or memory objects.

### Transfer platform experience rather than a skin

Direct manipulation, causal consistency, continuous feedback, quiet discoverable hierarchy, interruptible motion, steady reading rhythm and familiar controls are useful. Radius, font, color, blur and a particular curve are not principles. Motion should make changes understandable. Details, scope changes, saving, undo, return and recovery need legible results. Unknown loading remains honest; decorative loops do not create progress. Inspect keyboard focus, narrow windows, long content and reduced motion in affected transitions.

### Focused design review

- Can a first visit begin without reading setup instructions?
- Does a collapsed conversation retain a clear focus, with sources, boundaries and a next step still discoverable when expanded?
- Does memory begin with current useful content and a secondary entry?
- Are proposal, local registration, execution, uncertainty and undo truthful?
- Are connection, materials and preference settings grouped around understandable tasks?
- Can someone locate themselves in history, agenda or map and return?
- Do failure, stop and recovery preserve drafts and a real way forward?
- Have affected light/dark, narrow/full-screen, long-content and reduced-motion states actually been rendered and inspected?
- Can someone complete the core action without developer documentation?

Without user research, describe observations as heuristic or synthetic Electron evidence. Do not claim universal appeal or that users liked the result.
