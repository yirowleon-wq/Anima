## Experience and outcome

I want Sakiko to feel like someone sharing the room with me while I work, with her own attention and a recognizable way of responding. I should be able to begin quietly, bring a real study passage into the conversation, point to a line that is bothering me, and feel that she has actually understood which line I mean. If I correct her, the explanation and what she is attending to should change together. When I start writing, she should make room without making the whole page jump. When I use Back, a bookmark or Resume, I want to continue the same thought. Keep the full cover available through the existing brand navigation; returning to work and choosing the cover have different purposes.

Make that whole sequence worth using. Start with Sakiko and the reviewed scene and character assets in Anima's own manifest. Give her a composed, attentive presence: she can have an opinion, be curious, or wait quietly. Her personality should affect how she understands and helps, rather than being added to a finished answer as a warmer voice. I may want a demanding explanation, a small hint, or a moment with no task; let the actual conversation determine that.

The central experience should work with a real imported English passage or a saved note and a genuine editable study draft. I can indicate a passage or formula, ask about it, then correct the attention to a neighboring line. Keep the relationship between what I selected, what she explains and what is highlighted clear. If I begin typing or revising my own work, her gesture and emphasis should settle out of the way immediately. She should not carry on pointing at the old line, recite a fixed apology, or replace my attempt with her own answer.

Let me briefly open another relevant part of the app, put this aside and return through the appropriate continuation control. Preserve the same conversation, passage, draft and meaningful stopping point. Choosing a saved continuation should reopen the same work, with a natural change of attention from Sakiko rather than a reset. If nothing useful changed while I was away, I should simply be able to continue. If a source is no longer available or an operation fails, keep my writing and give me a plain, useful way forward.

Use image-led design to resolve the room, study composition and the return before implementing the final layout, reusing a suitable reviewed design when it already fits. Make the English text comfortable to read at a normal window size and a narrow one. Sakiko, her furniture, light and gestures should belong to the same space. Do not use green for the interface; natural foliage, the view outside and the characters' established clothing colors can remain. A difficult pose needs a sound new design, not a stretched or repeatedly repaired bad figure. Do not bring back retired images. The result must be a working interface, not a full-screen picture of one.

I want the motion to hold up when I change my mind halfway through. Looking toward the passage, preparing to explain, drawing back when I write, waiting and returning should form a small, complete performance. A temporary static fallback can keep the feature usable while an asset fails, but it does not finish a required gesture. Quiet audio, mute, reduced motion and keyboard operation should all remain useful. Do not make constant movement or repeated reassurance stand in for attention.

Keep Anima's existing conversation, materials, notes, scheduling, maps, interactive creations and character controls working. This is the first substantial unit of the shared-attention stage, not a replacement for those capabilities. Leave the other nine characters and their editable identities intact. Do not call the full stage, the character matrix, or the entire runtime redesign complete after this one sequence.
