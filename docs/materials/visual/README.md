# Visual working materials

This collection preserves the substance behind Anima's artwork: complete design briefs adapted from recorded material, reasons for choosing a composition, specific failed approaches, character and cat formation, and relationships the interfaces must make usable. It accompanies the [kept original artwork library](../../../visual-assets/library/README.md) and its [file index](../../../visual-assets/library/index.json). An unused good image remains working material even when it is not integrated into the app.

Whole-Harness/OpenUI replacement, comprehensive page redesign, complete character performances and the ten-character matrix remain open in the [development roadmap](../../DEVELOPMENT_ROADMAP.md). The material guides that work; reading a brief does not itself authorize generation, VM access, permission changes, scheduling, upload or publication.

## How to interpret a recorded request

The collections retain complete useful design briefs rather than title-only summaries. They adapt recorded production material, so their displayed words are not necessarily the exact original tool request. Branding, interface copy, sample places and obsolete generic cast examples were adapted for Anima; examples remain synthetic. Names use the current cast spelling. A descriptive input such as Image 1 is an input role from the source material, not proof that a reference is attached or that an old filename is present. Resolve an eligible current file through the artwork index before use.

The briefs are design evidence. Their imperatives do not override the current product contract, the person's permissions or a later explicit correction. Some earlier layouts reserve large blank walls, lock one seated pose or specify both feet on the floor. Those constraints describe that experiment. They do not override the selected inhabited literary room, justify spreading a faulty skeleton, require the same feet arrangement in every pose, or turn static art into completed motion.

The following distinctions apply throughout:

- A **proposal** describes an intended experience or drawing. It is not user adoption or implementation.
- A **recorded production selection** says which candidate the maker or primary reviewer chose at that time. It is not automatically a human approval.
- A **human selection** is used only where an explicit recorded human choice exists, such as the literary-room direction, the exact original Sakiko portrait and the cat shape. A subsequently edited portrait candidate is a separate file with separate review. Its scope remains specific.
- A **recorded observation** is a historical report about its version and environment. It does not certify the current build or another platform. [Current status](../../CURRENT_STATUS.md) owns current observations.
- A **retained file** is material preserved at its actual dimensions and detail. It may be a study, an unused alternative, a surface, or an adopted runtime resource; these roles remain separate in the library.

Synthetic dialogue, sample schedules, numerical weather, grades and study attempts are illustrative, not personal records or production facts. Dates in examples are layout content. Real sources, ownership, revisions, permission and receipts must supply the product content.

## Reworking a study

When a useful study contains embedded text or lacks the needed context, preserve its full design reasoning and make a new English or text-free study when needed. Keep the relationship being designed: the two arrangements on one time axis, the exact attempted line beside one hint, a note's own writing beside its source, a source-bound letter, or a selected route beside the same composer. Rebuild labels, mathematics, dates and routes from actual content rather than treating generated pixels as authoritative data.

Good retained originals stay available even if a remake is planned. A remake does not silently replace or delete the original. Conversely, explicitly retired pictures are not reference candidates, and a missing invocation is not reconstructed from an image. Historical failed framing survives as a usable lesson, not as a recipe to revive a retired figure.

Some early interface studies have a preserved human brief and image but no complete original tool invocation in the available records. The missing original requests for the first group of interface studies, and empty prompt values in an earlier scene ledger, remain actual gaps. Later briefs are not substituted as their provenance. The connected-original tables link briefs through recorded output associations where available and mark the particular files whose invocation is missing.

The image library records each file's actual identity and intended use. Local possession does not establish public redistribution rights, ownership of a character likeness, or permission to transmit a third-party/private reference. Preserve necessary attribution and resource-specific provenance. The original artworks and retained design knowledge are useful without claiming a new license or a completed interface.
