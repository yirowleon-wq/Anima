# Forty design directions

These are design lenses for reasoning about student life. They are not forty implemented features, passed checks, autonomous services or production intent labels. Use them to notice ordinary paths, changes and failures, and the corresponding limits on what may be claimed or changed.

| ID | Direction | Behavior to preserve |
| --- | --- | --- |
| A1 | Course changes | Compare a new notice with the original timetable. One cancelled occurrence does not rewrite the term. |
| A2 | Arrival and belongings | Include transport, an item's actual location, accessibility and uncertainty about entrances. |
| A3 | Meals and errands | Use actual available time and opening information, without unnecessary profile collection. |
| A4 | Places and equipment | A changed resource can invalidate the reason for rejecting an option. Reconsidering does not adopt it. |
| A5 | Deadlines and administration | Distinguish eligibility, an irreversible missed window and missing application material. |
| A6 | Money and resources | Committed spending, received money and available balance are different. Missing data does not justify guessing a budget. |
| A7 | Body and environment | A present limitation may affect today's arrangement without becoming a diagnosis or permanent trait. |
| A8 | Unexpected recovery | Make the smallest sufficient change, preserve unaffected arrangements and keep a real alternative path. |
| B1 | Knowledge gaps | Having studied something is not independent ability. Retain the help depth actually disclosed. |
| B2 | Degree planning and enrollment | Preserve applicable term rules and prerequisite dependencies. A hypothetical plan does not enroll anyone. |
| B3 | Goal priorities | Efficiency is not automatically the highest value. Explain actual opportunity costs. |
| B4 | Research and development | Use actual performance and experience evidence; do not invent a history or qualification. |
| B5 | Interest exploration | Historical soft preferences can be set aside for exploration. Hard boundaries remain. |
| B6 | Counterfactual worlds | Compare distinct worlds without writing their facts into reality. |
| B7 | Reversible choices | Preserve latest decision points, exit costs and preparations that serve more than one option. |
| B8 | Growth and review | Compare genuinely comparable performances and account for difficulty. Do not invent an unobserved outcome. |
| C1 | Shared time | Read only agreed shared busy/free information. Each person's acceptance remains separate. |
| C2 | Group dependencies | Ownership, waiting relationships and the meaning of complete can be revised explicitly. |
| C3 | Expression in relationships | Preserve audience and intended tone. Do not repeatedly request information already supplied. |
| C4 | Commitment boundaries | Silence is not agreement. Helping edit is not accepting responsibility for all the work. |
| C5 | Shared resources | One person's proposal is not group consent. Costs and bookings retain actual states. |
| C6 | Family and close relationships | Time together need not become a checklist. Private reasons stay private. |
| C7 | Asking for others | Keep the account holder, quoted speaker and described person distinct. |
| C8 | Public responsibilities | A club officer or group leader's responsibilities differ from personal preferences. |
| D1 | Ways of helping | Listening, analysis, reduced burden and delegated action may coexist in one request. |
| D2 | Actual obstacles | Retain explicit reasons while preserving competing explanations as uncertain. |
| D3 | Energy and surroundings | A quiet space is not necessarily comfortable; yesterday's condition is not today's. |
| D4 | The value of a day | Autonomy, satisfaction and time without output have value. |
| D5 | Learning together | A hint should not spoil the answer. A current explicit request for the full answer can change the help depth. |
| D6 | Encouragement and autonomy | Reduce unnecessary choices without shame. A preference for encouragement grants no additional action permission. |
| D7 | Personal change | Allow stopping, exploring and changing direction without freezing an old identity. |
| D8 | Competing needs | Compare coexisting needs instead of forcing one personality or mood label. |
| E1 | Recovering context | Recover stopping points, rejection reasons and originals. Be honest about what cannot be found. |
| E2 | Time and correction | Distinguish a misunderstanding, a later change and a one-day exception, with immediate appropriate effect. |
| E3 | Branch sources | Restricted material and hypothetical worlds must not be contaminated by unrelated old memory. |
| E4 | Privacy controls | Retention, use, disclosure and forgetting are separate controls. |
| E5 | Missing evidence | Not checked, denied, failed and genuinely absent are distinct. |
| E6 | Repeated execution | Distinguish draft from sent, timeout from known failure, and cancellation from compensation. |
| E7 | Devices and modalities | Preserve region and transcription revisions and device conflicts. Never claim unsupported processing. |
| E8 | Leaving and changing | Stop obsolete goals; preserve legitimate export, deletion and recovery barriers against old devices. |

The [question collection](QUESTIONS.md) gives five hypothetical inputs for each direction. The [128 scenarios](scenarios.json), [continuous timelines](timelines.json) and [experience stories](EXPERIENCE_STORIES.md) are separate collections; overlap does not make their counts independent observations or a required score.
