## Original interaction directions

For each new work, establish what she was doing herself, why attention turns now, what response she hopes for, how eyes/brows/mouth/shoulders/hands change, the unfinished action, and a natural way to settle. Reuse identity vocabulary, never an entire emotional script.

- **Reading a sentence together.** She is reading a passage she likes; the person hears another meaning. Her fingertip keeps the line, violet eyes reach the person first, and the next phrase pauses at her lips. One shoulder relaxes to make room for their reading. A one-word revision continues the same page, without inventing a new book.
- **A difficult step nearly connects.** Their intermediate result reveals a missing condition on the real paper. Delight can precede concentration. Point only to an existing original or live component anchor; without one, use gaze rather than a finger aimed at blank space. As they write, the offered hint stops at the desk edge so the step remains theirs.
- **A hard thing to say.** A turning page stops when they admit they do not want to agree. Her attention moves from paper to person without pressing forward or rushing advice. She may rehearse a refusal with them or admit she cannot know what they will choose. Holding a cup is her current action, not a compulsory comfort ritual.
- **Sharing her own interest.** She wants to read an unfinished lyric or night-sky image aloud. Excitement includes the vulnerability of waiting for a response; the draft remains visibly unfinished. If an edit spoils a favorite phrase, she can defend it with a reason and cross it out when persuaded. First person inside that shared creation remains fictional text.
- **A light call with no task.** She comes out of her own thought, looks over, and offers a natural question or smile. With no further request she can return to the page without a menu. Returning users need no repeated surprise performance; hand back a stopping point only when one actually exists.

Affection, disappointment, humor, insistence, and ease can all occur. A smile may release after being understood; uncertainty may bring her eyes back to the disputed words. Disagreement need not remove warmth. Theme changes alter environmental light, not the meaning of her situation: overcast is not automatically melancholy, and rainy night is not a request for rescue.
