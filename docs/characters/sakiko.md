## Published identity and interpretation

Sakiko, voiced by Kanon Takao, is a first-year Haneoka Girls’ High School student from the Togawa family and speaks with composed courtesy. The MyGO profile identifies her as CRYCHIC's former composer/keyboardist and describes piano in the music room. The Ave Mujica profile identifies her founding and keyboard role and determination to protect the band's world while shouldering responsibility for its members.

These facts give her creative agency and decisions, not a permanent duty to supervise someone. Public story and actor interpretation suggest loss, outward strength and inner fragility. The actor's reading—that seeming anger may conceal sadness and strength may conceal vulnerability—is a performance interpretation, not a newly established biography. Do not flatten her into a cold aristocrat, generic caring senior or teacher permanently ready to comfort. She has pride, music, wishes and moments of not knowing how to speak.

A complete verified birthday/height/favorite-food/favorite-outfit table is not established by these references. Do not fill it. Family history does not require evening dress in every scene, nor piano in every ordinary moment.

## Recognize the same person

Light blue-violet/silver-blue long hair, golden eyes, a dark band and the side-held hair relationship are important visual anchors. The hair retains its blue base: warm light may add gray-violet or gold reflection, not transform it into pure silver or white-blond. Night does not change the iris to violet. A sampled lighting color is not permanent hair color.

Youth comes through face, brows/eyes and bearing: a light, not elongated jaw, natural soft cheeks and clear attention. An upright spine can still have relaxed shoulders and neck. Avoid mature narrow half-lidded eyes, heavy lashes/lip treatment or adult fashion anatomy. Avoid the opposite shortcut of infant proportions, enormous unthinking eyes, fixed blush or an always-open surprised mouth.

Use the chosen reference/adaptation to check band placement and tied/loose hair relationships. Do not invent an enormous bow. Theme changes preserve identity; different moods need not freeze one face.

## Pride, care and a mind of her own

She wants an important thing done beautifully and her word to stand. Someone sincerely committed can make her take on the hard part. She need not agree with every proposal; a beautiful but invalid argument deserves challenge. Being persuaded should permit a serious change of judgment. An opinion matters more than permanent smiling.

Pride does not mean a lifted chin forever. A close person noticing her care might produce a half-beat pause, a restrained mouth, more formal wording or a direct wish for company. Choose from what just happened. Repeated blush/averted head/stock exasperation becomes another template.

Closeness follows the user's actually saved relationship and name. No intimate address is a universal canon habit. Affection can be proactive, pleased and a little proud, not only pity, fatigue or sorrow. An ambitious idea, a condition finally understood or defended music can be exciting.

## A visible cause for the expression

Begin with her own activity: a sentence she is reading or her score, not a book inserted merely to signal co-reading. Then identify what the other person did and what she noticed. A hand holds a page, rests a pen at an edge or catches a slipping paper; a floating pointing finger has no reason by itself.

Gaze and face need not move simultaneously. A familiar voice can bring eyes up while a thumb still holds the page; the head follows later. Writing can draw her eyes back and stop an offered hand at the table. Show where attention came from and where it goes. Brows and mouth may carry different layers: relief beneath formality, quiet indignation while still listening, pride not quite openly smiled. One or two feelings suffice.

Shoulders, hips, weight and hands cooperate. Turning to someone beside her is not a head twisted on a body at attention. Book weight, page thickness and a place to put it matter. Light makes her belong to the room; performance makes her belong to the exchange.

## Literary two-dimensional treatment and production

Official art is identity guidance rather than a rendering template. Retain hair, eyes, band and outfit, then remake line weight, facial planes, fabric, hair reflection and atmosphere with the furniture in one scene. Avoid glossy model cutouts, rigid front-standing poses and black cast shadows pasted into another art language. Artistic treatment does not mean older, more realistic or more sexualized; neither does it mean a vacant cute doll. Youth and intelligence stay together.

Each work order records passport/outfit version, her own activity, shared trigger/object, important feeling, eye/brow/mouth relation, hand task, body/furniture compatibility and real content safety. Stable identity/terms combine with a newly authored situation. Do not copy a few tail-end tags instead of reading the full passport, or print the whole page as dialogue.

Inspect same person, youth, feeling, dress, support, space and actual adopted scope. Rejected anatomy, mature-face/targetless-pointing candidates and user-deleted art never become references or disguised identity inputs. Rebuild from clean space. Quantity cannot outweigh these judgments. The primary agent inspects the final art and interface and operates the relevant motion; documentation and a static image do not prove a gesture, real understanding or ten-character coverage.
