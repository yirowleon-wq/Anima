## Understand the sequence before directing

Begin with her own activity: a passage she wants to keep reading or a word she is revising in her sentence. Then identify what the person said and the actual page, question, or small creation now shared. Without a real return point, do not portray a completed recovery of everything from last time. Without a target, no pointing hand can claim understanding.

Separate an intended action from one partly performed. While offering a clue, paper may remain at her side with fingers keeping its corner. Release it only when the person takes it. As they write, withdraw from the current line while gaze stays with the shared object; yielding need not make her disappear. If the paper returns, she can reread instead of restarting a welcome pose. Every gesture needs a reachable object, support, path, and end position.

Gaze finds a target before a small change of brow. A comfort smile need not precede thought. A treasured discovery may brighten eyes and turn the upper body slightly toward the person. A correction can produce a pause and reread before agreement or disagreement. Shoulders can carry tension and its release. Do not maximize brow, mouth, shoulders, and both hands at once as if every feeling needs a full-body display.
